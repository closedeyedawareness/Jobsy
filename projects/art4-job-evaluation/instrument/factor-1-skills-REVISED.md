# Factor 1 — Skills, revised: a search with considerations

**Status: PROPOSAL for review, 2026-09-03. Replaces the Skills section of
`factor-degrees.md` if adopted; that file is unchanged until it is.**

## Why the first draft was wrong

It anchored the degrees on one line — MBO / HBO / WO — as though required skill
were a single educational axis with jobs placed along it. Elmar's correction:
skill requirement follows **organisational requirement, departmental need, and
law**, and those do not collapse into an education level.

The examples that break the single line:

- A **registered accountant (RA/AA)** is not "WO-level". It is a protected
  title in a statutory register, with a continuing-education obligation. An
  employer cannot staff certain work without one — that is a legal
  precondition, not a rung on a ladder.
- **WFT** is not one certificate. There are several modules — Basis, Vermogen,
  Schadeverzekeringen particulier and zakelijk, Inkomen, Hypothecair krediet,
  Pensioen, Zorg — and each licenses advice in its own domain. Holding four is
  not "more educated" than holding one; it is **certified across more domains**,
  which is what lets the employer sell more. Breadth is its own quantity.

An education ladder cannot see either of those. It reads a role requiring three
WFT modules and a role requiring none as the same, if both say HBO.

## The considerations

A rater arrives at the degree by weighing these, not by reading one scale. They
are listed in the order that tends to settle the question fastest.

1. **Is a licence, registration or certification a LEGAL PRECONDITION for the
   work?** Not desirable, not preferred — may the employer lawfully have this
   work done by someone without it? (WFT for advice in its domain, RA/AA for
   statutory audit, BIG for reserved medical acts, the bar for advocacy.)
2. **Across how many distinct certified domains?** Each additional module or
   registration widens what the role may lawfully do. Breadth is separate from
   depth and must not be scored as depth.
3. **Is there a maintenance obligation?** PE/CPD hours, re-registration,
   supervised practice. A requirement that must be actively kept is a standing
   demand of the role, not a one-off entry condition.
4. **Depth of command within the domain** — the original axis, and still real:
   routine methods, non-routine cases, developing methods others follow.
5. **Whose requirement is it?** Statutory (the law), sectoral (a CAO or
   professional body), or organisational (this employer's own bar for the job).
   All three are legitimate; they are not equally objective and the difference
   belongs in the record.
6. **Formal qualification level**, LAST and as evidence rather than definition —
   it is a proxy, and proxies are where indirect discrimination lives.

## ⚠️ The neutrality problem this creates, stated before the degrees

Making certification visible raises the risk Art. 4(4) names in its final
sentence: *"In particular, relevant soft skills shall not be undervalued."*

Certified competence is disproportionately formalised in technical, financial
and regulated domains. Uncertified competence — coordination across teams,
de-escalation, client trust, care — is real, demanded, and has no register to
point at. A skills factor that rewards what can be evidenced by a certificate
will systematically underweight work that cannot, and in the Dutch labour market
that split is not gender-neutral.

Two consequences, both non-negotiable if this proposal is adopted:

- **A licence raises the degree only where it is genuinely required by the
  work.** A certification the role does not use is not skill; it is a fact about
  the person, and Art. 4 evaluates the job.
- **The absence of a certificate is never evidence of low skill.** Degrees 3
  and above must be reachable through demonstrated command alone. If in practice
  no uncertified role ever reaches degree 4, that is a finding about the
  instrument, and `separation()` will show it.

## Proposed degrees

| Degree | Definition |
|---|---|
| 1 | Task competence learnable on the job in weeks. No qualification or certification required to perform the work. |
| 2 | Working command of one domain's routine methods. Any certification is an entry formality rather than a standing licence to act. |
| 3 | Full professional command of one domain including non-routine cases **or** one legally required certification actively used and maintained. |
| 4 | Deep command of one domain **or** certified across several distinct regulated domains **or** a statutory registration carrying personal accountability and a maintenance obligation. |
| 5 | Recognised reference point in the domain; sets method for others. Where the domain is regulated, holds the senior registration and signs work others cannot. |
| 6 | Shapes the discipline across the organisation or beyond; where regulated, carries the accountability the register places on the individual rather than the employer. |

## Open for the session

- Degrees 3 and 4 both offer an "or" between command and certification. That is
  deliberate — it is the mechanism that keeps uncertified expertise reachable —
  but it means two roles at degree 4 can be there for different reasons. Is that
  acceptable, or should breadth-of-certification be a fifth factor under Art.
  4(4)'s "any other factors which are relevant"?
- The library holds none of this. Adopting it requires a qualifications
  dimension per role: certification, issuing body, statutory or not, required
  or desirable, maintenance obligation. That is a schema change, not a rating.
